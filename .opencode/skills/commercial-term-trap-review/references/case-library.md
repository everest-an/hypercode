# Case Library

All cases are anonymized. No company names, project names, unit numbers, addresses, people, emails, phone numbers, exact dates or uniquely identifying combinations.

Patterns are reusable. Numbers are illustrative.

## Case 001 — The one deleted word

**Pattern:** A renewal/pop-up term sheet looks almost identical to the prior version. The headline percentage is unchanged. One word disappears from a note.

**Old version (Year -1):**

- Rent: 8% of monthly turnover.
- Rent clause: "Tenant shall pay an additional 5% of rent as VAT."
- Note: "**Rent**, service fee and deposit include VAT."

**New version (Year 0):**

- Rent: 8% of monthly turnover.
- Rent clause: "Tenant shall pay an additional 5% of rent as VAT."
- Note: "Service fee and deposit include VAT."

**What changed:** The word "rent" was removed from the inclusive note.

**Why it matters:** The old note read as 8% inclusive (despite the contradictory VAT clause). The new note reads as 8% + 5% VAT, or about **8.4% effective**. The headline did not change. Only one word did.

**Signal:** A diff is not optional. Compare every note, every qualifier, every subject.

**Fix:**

1. Build the tax matrix.
2. Quote the prior version and the prior signed contract/bill.
3. Ask for one controlling sentence: "Rent is 8% of monthly turnover, inclusive of VAT; no additional VAT is payable."
4. If the counterparty refuses, price the difference and trade it for free rent, a service-fee cap, or a deposit reduction.

**Lesson:** The most expensive changes are often deletions, not additions.

## Case 002 — Pure percentage, all-in (positive pattern)

**Pattern:** A lease offers a single pure percentage rent, with no base rent and no minimum, stated as inclusive of tax and property fee.

**Term sheet:**

- Rent: 12% of monthly turnover.
- No base rent; no minimum guarantee.
- "Inclusive of tax and property fee."
- No separate service fee.

**Why it can be good:** The all-in occupancy cost is transparent if the definition of turnover and the inclusions are tight. There is no base-rent cliff in a low-sales month.

**What to check:**

- Does "turnover" include VAT, returns, discounts, shipping, gift cards, or platform fees?
- Does "property fee" include HVAC, water, electricity, cleaning and marketing?
- Is there any separate service fee, promotion fund or utility charge?
- Is the 12% applied to gross or net sales?
- Is there a minimum sales threshold or a guaranteed minimum?
- What happens on early termination? Is unamortized fit-out compensated?
- Is a special VAT invoice issued? Who bears the tax if the rate changes?

**Trap:** "Inclusive" can be diluted by a separate fee schedule. If the term sheet says "12% inclusive" but a schedule adds a property fee, the effective cost is 12% + property fee.

**Fix:** Attach a complete inclusions/exclusions schedule to the contract. Define net sales. Confirm no other mandatory fees.

**Lesson:** All-in pricing is only all-in if the inclusions are exhaustive.

## Case 003 — "No free rent" vs "fit-out rent-free"

**Pattern:** A term sheet says "免租期：无" (no rent-free period), then a note says "装修免租期内不需支付租金" (no rent during the fit-out rent-free period).

**Why it matters:** The two statements conflict. One reading: no free rent at all. Another: a fit-out period with no rent but property fee payable. The difference can be one or two months of rent.

**Signal:** The word "装修" (fit-out) appears in a note even though the main term says "none".

**Fix:** Require one sentence covering:

- start date (delivery or possession);
- length in days;
- rent payable or not;
- property/service fee payable or not;
- what happens if opening is delayed;
- whether the period is inside or outside the lease term.

**Lesson:** A contradiction is not a detail. It is a future dispute with a date attached.

## Case 004 — Service fee vs property fee

**Pattern:** A later, longer lease offer uses a "property management fee" at a much higher rate than the earlier pop-up's "service fee". Both are described as covering the mall's management.

**Why it matters:** The headline rent may look similar, but the fixed cost per square meter can be materially higher. The inclusion list may also differ: HVAC, water, electricity, cleaning, security, marketing.

**Signal:** A change in the **name** of the fee, not just the number. "Service fee" and "property fee" are not interchangeable.

**Fix:**

- Build a side-by-side inclusions table.
- Confirm what is included: AC, water, electricity, gas, cleaning, security, marketing, common area.
- Cap annual increases; require notice.
- Confirm whether the fee is tax-inclusive and what invoice is issued.

**Lesson:** Never assume the old fee schedule carries over when the fee is renamed.

## Case 005 — Three months of what?

**Pattern:** A deposit is described as "three months". The term sheet does not say whether it is three months of rent, three months of property fee, or both.

**Why it matters:** The cash tied up can differ by several times. The refund conditions may also differ.

**Signal:** "Three months" without a defined base.

**Fix:**

- Define the base: rent, property fee, or rent + property fee.
- State whether the deposit is tax-inclusive.
- State the refund period and any deductions.
- Limit application to undisputed unpaid rent.
- Confirm whether interest is paid.

**Lesson:** A deposit is a loan to the landlord. Price the carrying cost.

## Case 006 — Turnover definition

**Pattern:** A percentage rent clause says "8% of monthly turnover" but does not define turnover.

**Why it matters:** The landlord may include VAT, shipping, gift cards, returns, discounts or platform fees. The tenant may assume net sales.

**Signal:** "Turnover" or "营业额" with no formula.

**Fix:**

- Define net sales: gross sales excluding VAT, returns, refunds, discounts, shipping, gift cards, and sales taxes.
- Specify the POS/audit method.
- Confirm the reporting deadline and audit rights.
- Confirm whether online sales count, and at what rate.

**Lesson:** The percentage is only as good as the base it is applied to.

## Case 007 — Landlord break right

**Pattern:** "Landlord may terminate early on one month's notice."

**Why it matters:** The tenant may have spent on fit-out, staff and inventory. One month may not recover the investment.

**Signal:** A unilateral break right with no compensation formula.

**Fix:**

- Add a lock-in period.
- Add compensation for unamortized fit-out and relocation costs.
- Make the break right reciprocal, or narrow the triggers.
- Require a minimum notice period and a long-stop date.

**Lesson:** A short notice right is a transfer of option value from tenant to landlord.

## Case 008 — "As-is" handover and restoration

**Pattern:** "Premises delivered as-is; tenant removes hoarding and restores at expiry; tenant restores its own additions."

**Why it matters:** The cost of removal and restoration can exceed the deposit. The condition at handover may be disputed.

**Signal:** "现状交付", "围板拆除", "复原" in the same term sheet.

**Fix:**

- Sign a photo/video condition report at handover.
- Cap restoration cost or agree a fixed restoration fee.
- Clarify who removes the hoarding and who owns the removed materials.
- Define "normal wear and tear".
- Require landlord approval of restoration scope before work.

**Lesson:** Exit costs are part of the rent. Price them at signing.
